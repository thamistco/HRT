What this tool is for

Find Your HRT helps you see which HRT options might suit your situation, so you have an informed starting point for a conversation with a doctor or another prescriber. It is an information and preparation tool. It is not a diagnosis, a prescription, or a substitute for clinical judgement.

Last updated: 30 July 2026

Intended purpose

This is the plain statement of what this site is designed to do, and what it is deliberately designed not to do.

What it is intended to do

  • Explain, in plain English, what HRT is and what the main options are in the UK.
  • Ask you a short series of questions and, from your answers, show you a list of HRT options with the ones more likely to suit your situation shown first, each with an explanation of why.
  • Give you something concrete to take to an appointment, so the conversation starts further along than a blank page.
  • Serve as a quick reference for clinicians who want to see, at a glance, the range of options that might be discussed with someone in a given situation.
  • Point out where something in your answers means a clinician should look at it before HRT is started or changed.

What it is not intended to do, and does not do

  • It does not diagnose anything, including whether you are perimenopausal or menopausal.
  • It does not prescribe, and it does not tell you what to take. Doses shown are typical published starting points used as examples for discussion, not instructions.
  • It does not decide anything. Every option it shows is presented for you and a prescriber to consider, and the prescriber decides what is appropriate.
  • It does not know your full history. It sees only the handful of answers you give it, not your records, your medication list, your test results or your examination findings.
  • It does not replace an appointment, a clinical assessment, or your prescriber's judgement, and it must not be used to start, stop or change treatment on its own.
  • It is not a monitoring tool and provides no ongoing oversight of your treatment.

If something is urgent, do not use this site. For symptoms that need attention now, contact your GP, call NHS 111, or in an emergency call 999. This site cannot help in an emergency and is not monitored.

Where the content comes from

The content is based on current UK guidance, principally NICE guideline NG23 on menopause, British Menopause Society tools and guidelines, Women's Health Concern patient factsheets, and NHS information. Every source is listed on the site, with the version or review date it was taken from, so you can check anything for yourself.

Guidance is reviewed regularly and the site is checked against it for changes. The date of the most recent check is shown in the footer of every page. Guidance still changes between checks, and product availability in the UK changes more often than that, so a prescriber confirming against the current BNF or product information is always the final word.

This site is independent. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced in partnership with NICE, the British Menopause Society, Women's Health Concern, the NHS, or any manufacturer. Naming those organisations describes where the underlying guidance comes from; it does not imply they have reviewed or approved this site.

Products are named only because a prescriber and patient need to be able to talk about specific products, and the same clinical option is often listed with several brands.
